# Introduction to Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which biological fuels are oxidized in the presence of an inorganic electron acceptor, such as oxygen, to produce large amounts of energy, to drive the bulk production of ATP. 

## Key Stages
1. **Glycolysis**: Occurs in the cytoplasm; breaks down glucose into two molecules of pyruvate. Yields 2 ATP and 2 NADH.
2. **Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)**: Takes place in the mitochondrial matrix. It completes the breakdown of glucose by oxidizing a derivative of pyruvate to carbon dioxide.
3. **Electron Transport Chain (ETC)**: Located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. It produces the most ATP (approx. 34 ATP) by transferring electrons from NADH and FADH2 to oxygen.

## Importance
Cellular respiration is critical for the survival of most organisms because the energy in glucose cannot be used directly by cells. ATP is the usable form of energy.
